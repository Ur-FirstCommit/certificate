# Design direction

## Purpose and audience

The certificate portal gives participants in Beginner’s Paradise Hackathon a clear, trustworthy way to verify their email and save a record of what they accomplished. The audience includes first-time builders ages 13–21 and their families, so instructions should be direct, reassuring, and easy to follow.

## Visual concept

Treat the page like a small, considered keepsake: editorial typography, confident dark ink, and a quiet blue accent. The contrast between a large, expressive headline and a precise step-by-step claim card should make the process feel both celebratory and dependable.

The supplied reference image informs the background mood only. Use diffuse, cool ambient light—soft blue and faint cyan tones on a pale ground—to create depth around the content. Reinterpret the effect with different placement, scale, and layering. Do not copy the reference’s centered headline, text, button, layout, or exact gradient fields.

## Interface principles

- Keep email verification, name entry, and certificate download in a clear three-step sequence.
- Make the active step and the next action obvious; explain that the email determines award eligibility.
- Use a calm, restrained palette and reserve saturated blue for emphasis and progress.
- Prefer generous spacing, crisp borders, and subtle depth over heavy shadows or decorative clutter.
- Keep motion brief and optional; honor reduced-motion preferences.
- Preserve readable type, visible focus states, accessible form labels, and responsive layouts.

## Certificate

Generate a landscape certificate PDF with FirstCommit branding, the recipient’s chosen name, the certificate type, award title when applicable, event name, and issue date. Keep the document legible when printed and avoid implying a prize unless the private winner lookup returns an award.
